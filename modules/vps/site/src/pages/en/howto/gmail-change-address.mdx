## First: this changes the address — it doesn't add a mailbox

Google now lets you **change an existing account to a new @gmail.com address**. Afterwards:

- you still sign in to the **same** Google account — mail, Drive, photos and contacts are all there;
- **the old address keeps working** as an alias, so mail sent to it still arrives;
- you just get a nicer, easier-to-remember address — not a second, separate mailbox.

<Callout type="tip" title="Change the address, or create a new account?">
**Change it** if you simply dislike the current address, or it contains an old name or year.
**Create a new account** if you need a **completely separate** mailbox — for example one used only for a non-China Apple ID or a BandwagonHost account, kept apart from your main account. See [Create a Google account](/en/howto/gmail/).
</Callout>

## Steps

1. Open [myaccount.google.com/google-account-email](https://myaccount.google.com/google-account-email) in a browser and sign in. Or in your Google Account: Personal info → Email → Google Account email.
2. If your account is eligible, there's an option to change the address — open it.
3. Enter the new address (the part before @gmail.com); the page tells you whether it's taken. If so, try another.
4. Confirm as prompted — you may need to enter your password or complete 2-Step Verification again.
5. Afterwards, send yourself one email at the new address and one at the old address to check both arrive.

<Callout type="ok">
Your Google Account page shows the new address, and mail sent to both the new and old addresses lands in the same inbox.
</Callout>

## Limits

| Rule | Details |
|---|---|
| How often | Once a year, at most three times in total |
| Switching back | You can return to an address you used before |
| Old address | Becomes an alias that keeps receiving mail; nobody else can take it |
| Region | Launched in the US first in 2026, rolling out to other countries and regions |

## Before you change

<Callout type="warn" title="Sites that use your old address">
The old address still receives mail, but some sites use it as your username. After changing, you may still need to sign in to them with the old address; to switch them to the new one, change it in each site's settings.
</Callout>

- **Server provider, Apple ID, GitHub and similar accounts**: their login doesn't change automatically. Update the email in each account's settings if you want.
- **People who have your old address**: no need to tell them; it still receives mail. Share the new one when you like.
- **Sites you sign in to with "Sign in with Google"**: usually unaffected — it's the same account.

## Stuck?

<Q q="No option to change the address?">
Your account can't use the feature yet. Common reasons: not available in your region yet, a work or school account (controlled by an admin), already changed once this year, or already changed three times. Check again later, or just [create a new account](/en/howto/gmail/).
</Q>
<Q q="The address I want is taken?">
Try another: add numbers, initials and so on. Dots don't matter in Gmail (a.b@gmail.com and ab@gmail.com are the same address), so adding a dot doesn't make a new one.
</Q>
<Q q="Can I still sign in with the old address?">
Yes. Both addresses point to the same account; either works for sign-in.
</Q>
<Q q="Changed my mind?">
You can switch back to a previous address, but it uses up one of your changes (once a year, three in total).
</Q>
